DiCodec: continuous speech codec

Qualitative demo for reconstruction, zero-shot voice conversion, and continuous autoregressive TTS.

DiCodec is a continuous speech codec designed to represent speech with a global speaker embedding and a sequence of local continuous content tokens. Unlike discrete audio tokenizers, which trade acoustic fidelity for shorter token sequences, DiCodec targets ultra-low frame-rate continuous representations while preserving reconstruction quality and supporting downstream generation.

In this demo, the same speech samples are encoded with temporal compression ratios of 4x, 8x, and 16x, yielding local-token rates of 23.44 Hz, 11.72 Hz, and 5.86 Hz, reported below as approximately 23.5 Hz, 12 Hz, and 6 Hz. These settings illustrate how continuous local tokens can remain useful even when the latent sequence is compressed to only a few frames per second.

We evaluate reconstruction and TTS on LibriSpeech test-clean using UTMOS, dWER, and speaker similarity. For TTS, we add a small modular diffusion head that can be attached to any autoregressive backbone, and compare DiCodec against discrete-token baselines under the same evaluation protocol. DiCodec achieves the lowest dWER within the 25 Hz and 12.5 Hz groups, remains competitive at 6 Hz, and shows that continuous, disentangled tokens preserve intelligibility and speaker identity at very low temporal resolution. The TTS evaluation in this table is an intentionally extreme condition: we use a compact Llama 3 autoregressive model with approximately 45M parameters, 12 layers, 4 attention heads, character-level tokenization, and only 460 hours of training audio from the LibriSpeech clean-100 and clean-360 partitions. In this setting, the representation is especially stable as the frame rate decreases, suggesting that shorter continuous latent sequences make autoregressive modeling easier without collapsing acoustic quality.

Text-to-speech

For this qualitative TTS demo we use a stronger autoregressive diffusion model with approximately 200M parameters. The model generates 6 Hz continuous DiCodec tokens, using character tokenization and training only on the LibriSpeech clean-100 and clean-360 partitions. The tokens are decoded with the speaker embedding extracted from Sample 1. The table reports the 6 Hz TTS results.
